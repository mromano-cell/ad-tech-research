Weekly Narrative

This Week's TL;DR

Conversational advertising moved from experiment to performance channel this week. OpenAI/ChatGPT led all companies with 7 mentions, driven by new performance bidding, a reported 228% surge in ad spend, and growing scrutiny over how its ad agent operates. Meanwhile, Google pushed Gemini deeper into local discovery and CTV dominated Black Friday budgets, so the fight for high-intent consumer moments is now running on AI assistants, maps, and the living-room screen at the same time.

AI & Search

  • ChatGPT Ads added Target CPA and CPC bidding and expanded product campaign controls, a clear step toward performance-marketer parity with search platforms (Search Engine Land). Conversational advertising appeared in 5 articles and ChatGPT ads in 3 this week.
  • ChatGPT ad spending soared 228% in seven months, signaling fast budget migration into the channel (MediaPost). Transparency is lagging that growth: ChatGPT's agent writes its own queries to serve ads, and advertisers can't see them (Adweek).
  • OpenAI's new business agents give brands a free way into ChatGPT conversations, even as OpenAI sells that same conversational real estate as ad inventory (Digiday). OpenAI also rewrote its ChatGPT Business Hub terms after its legal language raised concerns (Digiday).
  • Google (6 mentions) is pushing AI into local discovery. Gemini is going local, and Google Maps can now create trip itineraries. Separately, Google clarified how AI Max URL exclusions work, which matters for advertisers trying to control where AI-driven search traffic lands.
  • Anthropic says no to ads, but Wall Street may have other ideas. For now, that leaves OpenAI and Google as the main AI search ads players (2 mentions).

Programmatic & Data

  • BADBOX 3.0, a CTV ad fraud scheme, is generating 40 billion bid requests a day. That scale is a serious supply-quality warning for programmatic buyers (Digiday).
  • New federal data shows apps like DoorDash and Uber Eats rely on a small group of superusers. This is a useful lens for anyone segmenting high-frequency, high-value local consumers.
  • The midterm elections offer a window into media buying for '28, previewing how political dollars will compete for local and CTV inventory (Digiday).
  • Adweek argues that the media RFP is quietly becoming obsolete, pointing to a shift toward more fluid, data-driven partner selection.

CTV & Video

  • CTV/OTT was the top ad format of the week, appearing in 8 articles. Brands boosted Black Friday CTV, retail and search spending (Digiday).
  • Amazon's Alan Moss explained how the company's Black Friday sports lineup and shoppable ads are winning advertisers, blending live sports reach with commerce (Adweek).
  • Twitch debuted shoppable, skippable ads as TwitchCon faced growing pains (Marketing Dive).
  • Google added API support for Responsive YouTube Ads, making it easier to scale video creative programmatically (MediaPost).
  • Brands are leaning into long-form entertainment for the holidays, including Geico's first holiday film, Verizon's Jonathan Bailey "Sexy Scrooge" spot, and Coke's 2026 holiday ad, which notably takes AI off the menu.

Retail Media

  • Amazon Ads led retail media mentions (3), anchored by its push for shoppable ads around Black Friday sports. Walmart Connect received 1 mention.
  • Adweek explores why airlines are suddenly racing to launch media networks, extending the commerce-media model beyond traditional retailers.
  • Gap Inc. is betting on "fashiontainment" to fuel growth, blending content and commerce (Digiday).
  • Digiday's D+ Research on 2026 holiday marketing tactics shows how brands are reallocating seasonal spend across retail, search, and CTV.

Attribution & Measurement

  • The IAB released an AI-era attribution blueprint, aiming to modernize measurement as AI agents mediate more discovery. Last-click, view-through, and ID solutions each appeared once this week (MediaPost).
  • Measurement gaps are surfacing in conversational ads because advertisers can't see the queries ChatGPT's agent writes. That makes query-level attribution and optimization harder.
  • Adweek looks at turning audience attention into better marketing decisions, reflecting continued interest in attention metrics as a complement to clicks.

Agencies

  • Digiday reports that hybrid won the in-housing debate, at least among the largest advertisers. Publicis received 1 mention this week.
  • Consulting firms are reviving their marketing ambitions in the C-suite, competing directly with holdcos for executive relationships (Digiday).
  • The creator economy (3 mentions) keeps absorbing production budgets, with more brands paying creators to make their ads (Digiday).
  • BuzzFeed tapped co-founder Chris Poydenis as revenue chief (Adweek).

Yelp Impact Analysis

The most direct competitive signal this week is Google pushing AI into local discovery, with Gemini going local and Maps generating trip itineraries. Both put AI-curated recommendations in front of consumers at the same high-intent moment where Yelp competes for restaurant and services searches. OpenAI is closing in on the same territory: Target CPA/CPC bidding, 228% spend growth, and free business agents make ChatGPT a credible performance channel for local businesses. However, the hidden-query issue gives Yelp an opening to position its transparent, consumer-initiated search intent and verifiable leads as a measurement advantage. The DoorDash/Uber Eats superuser data reinforces that a small, high-frequency cohort drives restaurant-category demand, which favors audience and loyalty targeting for multi-location restaurant clients. There was no notable news from Meta, Nextdoor, or Apple Business Connect this week. For programmatic and managed-service products, the IAB AI-era attribution blueprint and the BADBOX 3.0 fraud findings suggest multi-location advertisers will increasingly reward partners that offer brand-safe, fraud-resistant supply and clear store-level attribution.
