Weekly Narrative

This Week's TL;DR

AI is rapidly becoming the front door to high-intent discovery. Google AI Overviews now appear on 80% of branded queries, OpenAI is making ChatGPT Ads more measurable and visual, and AI agents increasingly control ad budgets. At the same time, Apple is squeezing third-party data out of iOS and Safari. For product marketers, platforms with first-party intent signals and provable outcomes are gaining leverage, while those that depend on open-web tracking or organic search traffic face growing pressure.

AI & Search

  • Search was the most-covered ad format this week, appearing in 7 articles. The headline stat: Google AI Overviews jumped from 26% to 80% of branded queries, a major shift in how users reach brand and business destinations.
  • Generative engine optimization (GEO) was a trending topic (2 articles). Adweek profiled 11 of the best-funded GEO startups racing to win AI search, a sign that visibility inside AI answers is becoming its own budget line.
  • OpenAI (3 articles) is moving to make ChatGPT Ads more measurable and more visual, as AI search advertising emerges as a distinct category.
  • Google (the most-mentioned company at 6 articles) continued expanding AI ad inventory. It opened AI Mode to prescription drug ads, a notable step into a heavily regulated vertical.
  • Google is also pushing advertisers toward demand-led budgeting, in which spend flexes with real-time demand rather than fixed caps. The change hands more budget control to Google's automation.

Programmatic & Data

  • Apple (5 articles) has far-reaching plans to block hundreds of programmatic data companies from iOS, which could significantly limit third-party data flows in mobile.
  • The impact is already visible. The ad industry is tallying early losses from Apple's Safari tracking block, reinforcing cookieless and ID solutions as trending attribution topics (2 articles each).
  • Google and the DOJ filed their proposed final judgments in the ad tech case, but key disputes remain unresolved. The outcome could reshape the ad server and exchange landscape.
  • T-Mobile is bringing its mobile data exclusively to Vistar to scale DOOH targeting. Carrier data is becoming a differentiator in DOOH, which appeared in 2 articles this week.
  • Agentic advertising is gaining momentum: AI agents are increasingly getting their hands on ad dollars, and TikTok broadened its agentic AI play and brought its global ad network to the US. However, some publishers still question the ROI of agentic advertising.

Retail Media

  • Amazon added Yelp Local Services to its shopping cart, extending its commerce footprint into local service discovery and booking.
  • Google Ads will automatically pull promotions from advertisers' websites. The change lowers the effort needed to run offer-driven ads and blurs the line between search and commerce media.
  • Retention is getting more attention. Adweek explored what it takes to transform first-time shoppers into repeat customers, reflecting a shift from pure acquisition to lifetime value.

Attribution & Measurement

  • Measurement is the bottleneck for AI ad channels. OpenAI's measurement gaps are keeping ChatGPT ads budgets at test level, which shows that new inventory alone won't unlock scaled spend.
  • Incrementality remains a priority. Adweek reported that trust and incrementality help brands scale bigger ideas.
  • Healthcare marketers are rethinking their infrastructure in search of real-time results, part of a broader push toward faster, outcome-based reporting in regulated verticals.
  • Signal loss from Apple (Safari tracking block) is increasing reliance on first-party data and modeled measurement across the ecosystem.

Agencies

  • PepsiCo's CEO explained the switch to Publicis as gaining "an important capability." Publicis was the only agency to trend this week (1 article).
  • Synthetic audiences are taking off. Digiday's Media Buying Briefing reports that synthetic audience adoption by agencies and clients is accelerating, letting teams use AI-simulated consumers for planning and testing.
  • An AI-driven cottage industry is forming around Madison Avenue, while AI is changing how marketing and technology teams work together inside brands and agencies.
  • The creator economy is professionalizing. Universities are building creator economy programs, and top creators are taking fewer brand deals, with influencer marketing trending in 2 articles.

Yelp Impact Analysis

The most directly relevant news this week is Amazon adding Yelp Local Services to its shopping cart, which opens a new commerce-native distribution channel for Yelp's services and home-services advertisers and should be positioned as proof of Yelp's value as a high-intent local demand engine. On the competitive front, Google is tightening its grip on discovery: AI Overviews now appear on 80% of branded queries, which could reduce click-through to third-party listings, and features like auto-pulled website promotions and demand-led budgeting make Google's offering more hands-off for SMBs and multi-location brands. Meanwhile, OpenAI/ChatGPT ads are a rising AI-search competitor, but measurement gaps are keeping budgets at test level. That gives Yelp an opening to differentiate on closed-loop outcomes such as leads, calls, and reservations, and to treat GEO as a priority so Yelp content is cited inside AI answers. Finally, Apple's planned iOS data restrictions and Safari tracking block will erode third-party targeting for competitors that rely on open-web data. This raises the value of Yelp's first-party intent signals for programmatic and managed-service products sold to multi-location restaurants and service businesses. It also warrants close monitoring of Apple Business Connect as Apple consolidates control of local discovery on iOS.
